 proximal part of a FISTA–Chambolle–Dossal iteration. The resulting potential-positive conser-
 vative proximal map again reduces to a scalar mass multiplier and independent monotone nodal

 a high-accuracy KKT refinement. When the discrete state is interior, the full target Hessian is
 symmetric positive definite; we eliminate the mass multiplier by a Schur complement and solve
 the resulting Hessian systems by preconditioned conjugate gradients with a variable-coeﬀicient
 finite-difference preconditioner. A primal–dual active-set solver is retained only as a fallback
 when an active inequality constraint is detected.
     The numerical study separates potential-regularization bias, Fourier truncation, optimiza-
 tion, and box-tail errors. In particular, the spatial state error is measured by comparing ρN with
 the Fourier projection PN ρref , while the unresolved reference tail is quantified independently by
 Parseval’s identity. The dependence of the far field on the smoothing scale σ is also examined in
 order to identify a regime that suppresses artificial active sets without introducing a dominant
 box-tail error.

    A diﬀiculty common to fixed regularizations for which rε (0) > 0 is that the regularized kinetic

For large V (x) this linear slope is compatible with an obstacle KKT condition and can generate a
large discrete active set. Such a hard support boundary limits the regularity of the trigonometric
interpolant even when the denominator regularization itself is smooth. We therefore regularize the
potential contribution consistently at very low density by replacing ρ with

This map is nonnegative, convex, converges uniformly to ρ as σ → 0, and satisfies p′σ (0) = 0. The
scale σ is tied to the kinetic regularization scale through a prescribed law, for example σ = εp , so
no independent physical parameter is introduced.
    The potential smoothing creates a second numerical issue. Since p′′σ (0) = 1/σ, a very small
σ makes the potential term extremely stiff if it is included in the smooth gradient used by an
accelerated first-order method. The key algorithmic step is therefore not to change the target
energy, but to change its splitting: the regularized kinetic, β, and HOI-gradient terms form the
smooth part, while the smoothed potential together with positivity and mass is placed in the
proximal part. This produces a convex, separable potential-positive conservative prox with only
one global scalar mass multiplier.
    High spatial accuracy also requires a clean separation between energy minimization and final
stationarity. In our computations the FISTA stage typically determines the target energy to nearly
machine precision well before the projected-gradient residual reaches the level needed for spectral
comparisons. We therefore monitor the energy over a short window and hand the state to a KKT
refinement once the energy is stationary and the iterate has entered a local regime. For the interior
states produced by the potential smoothing, the full target Hessian is symmetric positive definite.
Rather than solving an indefinite KKT saddle system by GMRES, we eliminate the mass multiplier
through a scalar Schur complement and solve two SPD Hessian systems by PCG with a variable-
coeﬀicient finite-difference preconditioner. The latter is used only as a preconditioner; the target
discretization remains Fourier pseudospectral.
    The main contributions of this work are therefore:

 • a two-function convex regularization framework, with an admissible positive concave denom-
   inator rε for the density kinetic term and a convex vacuum-compatible potential map pσ ,
   together with a fixed-box Γ-convergence result as ε → 0 and σ(ε) → 0;

 • a Fourier pseudospectral density discretization with exact discrete mass conservation and pos-
   itivity at the collocation points;

 • an equivalence between the constrained spectral L2 projection and a weighted nodal positive
   conservative projection;

 • a potential-proximal FISTA-CD iteration that removes the large curvature V p′′σ from the
   smooth backtracking model while retaining the same convex target energy;
 • an energy-window handoff to a high-accuracy KKT refinement, with an interior Newton–
   PCG solver exploiting the SPD target Hessian and a PDAS solver retained only for active-set
   fallback;
 • a spectral-space error decomposition that compares ρN with PN ρref and separates the resolved-
   mode error from the unresolved reference Fourier tail, together with explicit diagnostics for
   the far-field and box truncation.

the convex kinetic–potential regularization, and its fixed-box variational convergence. Section 3
introduces the Fourier pseudospectral discretization and the positive mass-conservative projection.
Section 4 develops the potential-proximal FISTA iteration and the Newton–PCG KKT refinement.
Section 5 reports the numerical experiments, and Section 6 concludes the paper.

6       Conclusion
We have developed a convex Fourier pseudospectral framework for the density formulation of Bose–
Einstein condensates with higher-order interactions. The method is formulated in terms of two
scalar regularization maps: a convexity-preserving regularization of the density kinetic term through
an admissible positive concave denominator rε , and a convex vacuum-compatible potential map pσ .
The classical shift rε (r) = r + ε and the square-root potential smoothing are concrete members
of this framework. The structural assumptions on the two maps preserve convexity, and the joint
regularized energies converge variationally to the original fixed-box density energy as their scales
vanish.
    At the discrete level, the method preserves mass exactly and imposes positivity at the Fourier
collocation points. The constrained spectral L2 projection is equivalent to a nodal positive con-
servative projection. The same multiplier structure extends to the nonlinear potential prox. This
makes it possible to place the stiff smoothed potential in the proximal part of FISTA-CD without
changing the target energy. The first-order stage is terminated by an energy-window criterion once
it has entered the local regime, and the final KKT accuracy is obtained by an interior Newton–PCG
method that exploits the SPD target Hessian. A variable-coeﬀicient sparse finite-difference Hessian
approximation provides an effective preconditioner while the target discretization remains spectral.
    The remaining numerical issue is not the existence of a discrete active set but the interaction
between the potential smoothing scale and the far field. The scale law σ = εp must be chosen
small enough to reduce the perturbation of the linear potential and the box tail, while remaining
numerically tractable. The final numerical study therefore treats the smoothing-scale, spatial,
optimization, and box errors separately rather than combining them into a single refinement table.
    A natural next theoretical step is a joint analysis of box truncation, Fourier approximation,
and the coupled limit rε → id, σ(ε) → 0. Another question is to characterize how the choice of the
admissible regularization denominator affects regularity and conditioning, and when the potential
